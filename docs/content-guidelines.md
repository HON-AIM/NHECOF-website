# NHECOF Content Guidelines

> **Version:** Draft — for internal review
> **Last updated:** September 2025

These guidelines help NHECOF contributors create clear, accurate, and accessible content for the website.

---

## General Principles

1. **Accuracy first.** Every claim must be supported by a credible source.
2. **Clear language.** Write for a general audience. Avoid jargon or explain it.
3. **Nigerian context.** Use local foods, measurements, and examples where possible.
4. **Accessible.** Use plain English, short paragraphs, and descriptive headings.
5. **Honest.** Never exaggerate benefits or make medical claims.

---

## Writing Style

### Tone

- **Credible** — we are a trusted source of nutrition information.
- **Human** — write as if speaking to a community member.
- **Empowering** — help readers make informed choices.
- **Respectful** — avoid patronising language.

### Language

- Use British spelling (e.g. "colour", "organisation", "programme").
- Use `en-NG` conventions for dates and numbers.
- Avoid marketing language: no "game-changing", "revolutionary", or "miracle".
- Write in active voice where possible.

### Formatting

- Use **heading hierarchy** (H2 → H3 → H4). Never skip levels.
- Keep paragraphs short (3–4 sentences maximum).
- Use bullet points for lists of 3+ items.
- Bold key terms on first use.
- Include at least one Nigerian/local example per article.

---

## Article Guidelines

### Structure

Every article must include:

1. **Title** — clear, specific, under 80 characters.
2. **Summary** — 2–3 sentences, under 300 characters.
3. **Key Takeaways** — at least 2 bullet points.
4. **Body** — structured with H2/H3 headings.
5. **References** — at least 1 authoritative source.
6. **Author and Reviewer** — named individuals with qualifications.

### Evidence

- Cite sources inline and in the references section.
- Use recent sources (within 5 years where possible).
- For Nigerian-specific claims, use Nigerian studies when available.
- Link to original sources, not summaries.

### Prohibited Content

- Medical diagnoses or personalised treatment advice.
- Claims that a food or supplement "cures" or "treats" a disease.
- Unqualified health recommendations.
- Content targeting vulnerable groups without appropriate review.

---

## Food Profile Guidelines

### Structure

Each food profile should include:

1. **Common name** (and scientific name if appropriate).
2. **Alternative names** (local names, dialect names).
3. **Description and common Nigerian uses.**
4. **Key nutrients** (with amounts where available).
5. **Evidence-supported benefits.**
6. **Healthy preparation options.**
7. **Practical portion context.**
8. **Cautions and allergies** (mandatory — enter "None known" if applicable).
9. **Affordable meal ideas.**
10. **References.**

### Rules

- Never describe a food as curing a disease.
- Cautions must always be filled in.
- Use Nigerian food names and measurements.
- Include practical, affordable preparation suggestions.

---

## Image Guidelines

### Requirements

- Use **authentic photographs** from NHECOF activities where possible.
- No stock photos of people without consent.
- Every non-decorative image must have **alt text**.
- Every image must have a **consent status**:
  - `cleared` — publication permitted
  - `pending` — awaiting consent
  - `not-for-publication` — do not publish

### Minors

- Any image showing a person under 18 requires **guardian consent**.
- Set `containsMinors: true` and `guardianConsentRecorded: true`.
- The system blocks publication if guardian consent is not recorded.

### Technical

- Minimum width: 1200px for featured images.
- Prefer landscape orientation for hero images.
- Include credit/photographer name.

---

## Quote Guidelines

- Maximum 220 characters.
- Must include attribution (author or "NHECOF").
- Must be factually accurate and appropriately sourced.
- No AI-generated health quotations without qualified review.
- Set `sharingPermitted` explicitly.

---

## Outreach Record Guidelines

Every outreach record must include:

- Title and theme.
- Date and location (community, LGA, state).
- Summary and objectives.
- Target population.
- Activities conducted.
- Attendance and screening numbers.
- Key results (quantifiable where possible).
- Verification by an outreach manager.
- Photographs with consent status.

---

## Review Checklist

Before submitting content for review, verify:

- [ ] Title is clear and under 80 characters.
- [ ] Summary is under 300 characters.
- [ ] At least 2 key takeaways (articles).
- [ ] All health claims are referenced.
- [ ] Cautions field is filled (food profiles).
- [ ] Author and reviewer are assigned.
- [ ] Images have alt text and consent status.
- [ ] No prohibited medical language.
- [ ] British spelling is used throughout.
- [ ] Local Nigerian examples are included where relevant.
